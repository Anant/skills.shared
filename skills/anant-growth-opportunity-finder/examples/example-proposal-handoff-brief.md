# Example: Proposal Handoff Brief — Intelcraft

*(Illustrative example based on a fictional opportunity, labeled [ILLUSTRATIVE]. A real brief must reference a verified opportunity with a primary-source link.)*

## Opportunity Summary
- Opportunity: [ILLUSTRATIVE] Responsible AI Education Fund
- Funder: [Foundation A]
- Program: Responsible Technology / AI Literacy
- Deadline: Rolling (verified [date] at [link])
- Link: [primary source link]
- Funding amount: Up to $50,000, 12 months
- Applicant: Intelcraft (via fiscal sponsor)
- Recommended apply / no-apply decision: **Apply — composite 86/100; strong fit, Level 2 effort**

## Strategic Recommendation
- Recommended project title: "AI Literacy for Working Professionals: A Responsible-AI Curriculum and Community Program"
- Recommended project framing: Practical, community-anchored AI education that builds responsible-AI habits in the professionals who deploy AI daily
- Best-fit funding thesis: AI literacy and workforce development
- Why this funder is a fit: Explicitly funds AI literacy curriculum; recent grantees include practitioner-education programs of similar size
- Why this applicant is credible: Operating pilot with cohorts delivered, open knowledge base in use, testimonials from professional participants
- Why now: AI adoption in professional workplaces is outpacing responsible-use capacity; the funder's current cycle names this gap

## Funder Priorities
- Stated priorities: AI literacy, responsible adoption, practitioner education, open resources
- Target demographic: Working professionals, nonprofit staff, public-sector teams
- Review criteria: Clarity of learning outcomes; reach; sustainability; openness of materials
- Keywords to mirror: "responsible AI," "AI literacy," "practitioner," "open educational resources," "capacity building"
- Phrases to avoid: "disruption," pure "productivity gains" framing without responsibility framing
- Evidence the funder expects: Participation counts, learning-outcome measures, participant testimonials

## Project Alignment
- Relevant applicant work: Pilot cohorts, study halls, office hours, knowledge base
- Relevant project components: Curriculum modules, train-the-trainer path, OER publication
- Relevant beneficiaries: Nonprofit staff, public-sector teams, small business operators, educators
- Relevant outcomes: Responsible-AI competency, workflow adoption, community capacity
- Relevant partners: Fiscal sponsor; community organizations hosting cohorts
- Relevant prior proof: Pilot participation and testimonials (attach)

## Proposal Narrative Direction
- Core problem: Professionals are adopting AI faster than they are learning to use it responsibly, creating quality, governance, and equity risks
- Proposed solution: Applied AI-literacy curriculum + cohort delivery + open knowledge base
- Main activities: Develop 6 modules; run 4 cohorts; publish OER; train 10 facilitators
- Expected outputs: Curriculum, cohorts delivered, facilitators trained, OER downloads
- Expected outcomes: Measured competency gains; responsible-use practices adopted in participant workplaces
- Evaluation approach: Pre/post assessments, 90-day follow-up practice survey
- Sustainability: Facilitator network + earned-revenue workshops + open materials
- Scalability: OER + train-the-trainer replication
- Equity / access framing: Sliding-scale seats; nonprofit/public-sector priority; open materials remove cost barriers
- AI responsibility / governance framing: Governance and evaluation habits embedded in every module, not a bolt-on ethics unit

## Budget Direction
- Recommended request amount: $50,000
- Eligible costs: Curriculum development, facilitation, evaluation, OER production
- Costs to avoid: Capital, lobbying
- Budget categories: Personnel 60% / contractors 20% / program 10% / evaluation 5% / indirect 5%
- Match requirement: None stated (verify)
- Indirect cost notes: Confirm sponsor's rate vs. funder cap

## Required Materials
- Narrative: 3 pages (adapt pilot report)
- Budget: 1 page + justification
- Work plan: 12-month table
- Letters of support: 2 (community partner, participant employer)
- Organizational documents: Fiscal sponsor agreement
- Financial documents: Sponsor's financials
- Attachments: Curriculum outline, testimonials
- Registration requirements: Funder portal account only

## Risks and Gaps
- Eligibility concerns: Confirm fiscal-sponsorship acceptance in writing
- Missing documents: Formal evaluation plan (draft exists — finalize)
- Weaknesses: Early-stage track record; mitigate with pilot data
- Questions to resolve: Indirect cap; OER licensing requirement details
- Compliance issues: None identified
- Deadline risk: Low (rolling) — but funds may exhaust; submit within 2 weeks

## Drafting Instructions for Proposal Workflow
Use this opportunity brief to draft a tailored proposal. Mirror the funder's language. Emphasize the applicant's strongest fit with the stated priorities. Do not invent facts, partners, outcomes, or financial details. Clearly mark assumptions and placeholders.
