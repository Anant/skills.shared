# International Funding Sources

> **Verification reminder:** International programs carry extra eligibility complexity (country lists, currency, tax treaties, transfer restrictions). Verify applicant-country eligibility explicitly, and whether US entities (or your jurisdiction) can apply or must partner locally.

## Categories

### Multilateral and intergovernmental programs
- **Useful for:** Digital development, digital public infrastructure, education at scale
- **Typical opportunities:** Contracts, cooperative programs, innovation funds
- **Complexity notes:** Level 4–5; procurement-style processes
- **Where to look:** UN-system innovation funds, development banks' digital programs, UNESCO-adjacent education/AI-ethics initiatives. [verify per body]

### Bilateral development agencies
- **Useful for:** Digital skills and governance programs in partner countries
- **Complexity notes:** Level 4–5; usually require in-country partners
- **Where to look:** major bilateral agencies' digital development and innovation windows. [verify per agency]

### European programs
- **Useful for:** Research consortia (AI, trustworthy tech), open-source infrastructure (see NLnet/Sovereign Tech in the open-source file), digital rights
- **Complexity notes:** EU research framework programs are Level 5 and consortium-based; EU-funded regranting programs (often via foundations) can be Level 2–3
- **Where to look:** EU research/innovation portals, EU-backed internet-commons funds. [verify]

### International foundations and philanthropies
- **Useful for:** Global AI governance, digital rights, information ecosystems
- **Where to look:** international philanthropies with technology & society portfolios; global education funders. [verify per funder]

### Global prize platforms and challenges
- **Useful for:** Open-eligibility competitions (see grant-databases.md prize section)

### Country-specific AI and digital sovereignty funds
- **Useful for:** Local AI capacity, language technology, sovereign compute initiatives
- **Where to look:** national digital agencies and AI strategies — most publish funding instruments. [verify per country]

## Screening Questions
1. Is the applicant's country/entity type eligible, or is a local partner required?
2. Currency and payment mechanics? Withholding taxes?
3. Reporting language and standards?
4. Does the funder require in-country activities or beneficiaries?
5. Data localization/sovereignty requirements relevant to AI projects?
